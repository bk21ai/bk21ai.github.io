기조 강연 Keynote
신기정 교수
Prof. Kijung Shin
KAIST
AI for Complex Networks: With a Focus on Hypergraphs
Talk Overview

Talk Overview: AI for complex networks supports a wide range of applications, including web search, recommendation, advertising, and fraud detection, across the web, online social networks, and e-commerce platforms. However, it also poses unique challenges due to the massive scale but limited number of such networks, as well as their structural properties, such as permutation invariance. In this seminar, I will present our recent research on AI for complex networks, focusing on hypergraphs that represent interconnected group interactions. The seminar will cover embedding, generation, and modeling of large-scale group interactions. I will also briefly discuss broader directions, including complex networks for AI and AI for complex systems beyond networks.

Bio

Kijung Shin is an Associate Professor in the Kim Jaechul Graduate School of AI at KAIST. He received his Ph.D. in Computer Science from Carnegie Mellon University in 2019 and his B.Eng. in Computer Science and Engineering from Seoul National University in 2015. His primary research interests include complex networks, graph algorithms, and data mining. He has published more than 100 peer-reviewed papers in top-tier AI and data-related venues, and his work has been recognized with the IEEE ICDM Best Paper Award in 2025, the IEEE ICDM Best Student Paper Runner-up Award in 2023, and the ACM SIGKDD Best Research Paper Award in 2016. He also received the KIISE/IEEE Young Computer Researcher Award in 2025 and the Songam Distinguished Research Award in 2022.

Session 1 · Talk 1
나기현 박사
Dr. Kihyun Na
한동대학교
Handong Global University
Decouple, Don’t Denoise: Rethinking Conditioning in Diffusion Models - A Case Study in Virtual Try-On
Talk Overview

Conditional diffusion models often inject external information directly into the denoising process, but this design can blur the line between what the model should generate and what should merely serve as conditioning. In this talk, I examine this problem through diffusion-based virtual try-on, where the goal is to synthesize a person wearing a target garment. Recent dual-UNet methods achieve strong garment fidelity using a separate reference network, while simpler spatial-concatenation methods are more efficient but have struggled under full fine-tuning. I show that this failure stems from a conditioning–denoising conflict, in which the garment is treated both as a denoising target and as a conditioning signal. Building on this diagnosis, I introduce DeCo-VTON, a simple recipe that restores decoupling through Garment-Free Guidance, Decoupled Loss, and Clean Latent Anchoring. Without any architectural modification, DeCo-VTON enables effective full fine-tuning and reaches state-of-the-art performance among single-network methods at substantially lower computational cost.

Bio

Kihyun Na is a Research Professor in the BK21 AI Education & Research Group at Handong Global University. He received his Ph.D. in Computer Science and Engineering from Handong Global University, where his dissertation focused on garment feature learning in diffusion-based virtual try-on. His research focuses on generative computer vision, diffusion models, image restoration, and efficient AI systems for complex real-world data. His recent work studies how conditioning mechanisms in diffusion models can be redesigned to improve controllability and computational efficiency. He has also worked on license plate image restoration and recognition, audio and speech processing, and AI-assisted research workflows, with a broader interest in building reproducible AI systems that support human understanding and decision-making.

Advancing Multimodal Medical AI through Global Research Collaboration
Talk Overview

Multimodal medical AI has become an increasingly important research direction as clinical applications require the integration of diverse data sources, including medical images, electronic health records, physiological signals, and text. In this talk, I will share our three-year collaborative research experience with Prof. Yuyin Zhou at the University of California, Santa Cruz, supported by the IITP Global Research Support Program in Digital Fields. Each year, two Handong graduate students have visited the UCSC Computer Science Department as visiting researchers for four to six months. I will introduce the research outcomes from this collaboration, including papers, abstracts, domestic publications, and media coverage, with several results presented or submitted to top-tier international venues in medical AI, machine learning, and neuroscience. I will also discuss how this collaboration has contributed to student growth, research direction, and future opportunities in multimodal medical AI.

Bio

Charmgil Hong is an Associate Professor in the School of Artificial Intelligence, Computer and Electrical Engineering at Handong Global University. He received his Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Pittsburgh in 2017. His recent research focuses on multimodal and multi-source machine learning for complex real-world data, including time-series, image, video, and text data. His work connects anomaly and outlier detection, video understanding, large-scale video indexing, LLM-based discourse analysis, and AI-driven decision support, with applications in medical and industrial domains. His work has been recognized with the IEEE AVSS Best Paper Award, the AAAI Doctoral Consortium Grant, and travel grants from NSF, SIAM, and ICML. He currently serves as a technical advisor to several AI and healthcare technology companies, including Impactive AI, Claimark, AI Cloud, Domingo Research, and Herings.

From Understanding Human Videos to Socially Intelligent Agents
Talk Overview

This talk presents a series of studies on understanding collective human behaviors in videos, spanning group activity recognition, group activity detection, and fine-grained social interaction detection. Understanding human collective behavior requires compositional reasoning over multiple levels - from individual body-part cues such as gaze, gesture, and facial expression, up to pairwise interactions and group-level dynamics. Ultimately, such understanding forms the foundation for building socially intelligent systems that can perceive and reason about human activities in the real world. Looking ahead, I will discuss two future directions. The first is multimodal social activity understanding, extending beyond visual cues to incorporate language and audio for richer scene interpretation. The second is learning from human videos for robotic manipulation, which leverages large-scale human data to teach robots dexterous skills, ultimately moving toward multi-agent collaboration in complex environments.

Bio

Dongkeun Kim received his Ph.D. in Computer Science and Engineering from Pohang University of Science and Technology (POSTECH) in 2025 under the supervision of Prof. Suha Kwak. He is currently an Assistant Professor at Handong Global University, School of Artificial Intelligence, Computer and Electrical Engineering. His research interests include computer vision, video understanding, and multimodal learning with a particular focus on group activity understanding and social activity analysis. He received the Best Paper Award (Gold Prize) at IPIU 2026.

Session 2 · Talk 2
최희열 교수
Prof. Heeyoul Choi
한동대학교
Handong Global University
Sentence Segmentation and Named Entity Recognition in Literary Sinitic Text
Talk Overview

It is challenging to understand Literary Sinitic text from the Joseon dynasty, since there is a lack of explicit word separators, which creates significant semantic ambiguity. To address this, both sentence segmentation and named entity recognition (NER) are essential. We propose a Transformer-based analyzer that performs these two tasks simultaneously. Trained on a labeled corpus from the Seungjeongwon Ilgi, our model effectively segments sentences and identifies named entities, thereby significantly improving the understanding of sentence structure and overall context.

Bio

Heeyoul Choi received his B.S. and M.S. from Pohang University of Science and Technology (POSTECH), Korea, in 2002 and 2005, respectively, and his Ph.D. from Texas A&M University in 2010. He is a professor at Handong Global University. His research interests span machine learning, deep learning, and natural language processing.
